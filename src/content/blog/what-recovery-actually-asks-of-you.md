People preparing for surgery tend to plan the operation in detail and the fortnight afterwards
hardly at all. It is the wrong way round. The operation is a morning. The recovery is the part you
actually live through, and it goes better when it has been arranged in advance rather than
improvised from the sofa.

## Time, honestly

You will be given a return-to-work estimate at your consultation, specific to the operation and to
what you do for a living. Take it seriously and then add a margin. Desk work and lifting work
recover on quite different timetables, and so do two people having the same procedure.

The useful question to ask is not "when can I go back" but "when can I do the particular things my
week is made of". Driving, carrying a toddler, a full night's sleep, a gym session, a long flight:
each has its own answer, and they are not all the same answer.

## Help, specifically

For the first few days you want somebody else doing the lifting, the cooking and the school run.
Arrange it before, not after. If there is nobody obvious, say so at your consultation: the practice
works with companies that provide support at home, and where an overnight stay would be more
comfortable than going home the same day, the facility can offer that too.

## The bit nobody warns you about

Swelling and bruising look worse before they look better, and they peak when the adrenaline of
having got through it has worn off. Somewhere around the end of the first week it is common to
feel flat, to look in the mirror and wonder what you have done, and to be quite sure the result is
wrong. It is almost always too early to tell.

Shape settles over months, not days, and scars take a year or more to fade. Knowing that in
advance does not stop the feeling, but it does stop you acting on it.

## Follow-up is not optional

You will be seen, and seen by Mr Shiatis rather than by a delegate. Those appointments are how a
problem gets caught early, while it is still small and easily dealt with. If something worries you
between them, ring; nobody minds, and the call that turns out to be nothing is still the right call.
