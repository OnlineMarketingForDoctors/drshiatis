Most people arrive at a first consultation braced for a sales meeting. It is worth saying plainly
what this one is instead, because knowing the shape of the hour makes it easier to use.

## You talk first, and for a while

The appointment opens with a conversation across a desk, fully dressed. Mr Shiatis will ask what
you would like to change, and he means in your own words rather than in the name of an operation.
People often arrive having already decided which procedure they want, usually from something they
have read. That is a perfectly reasonable place to start, but the result you are describing and the
operation you have named are not always the same thing, and it is better to find that out now.

Expect questions about your general health, your medicines, previous surgery, whether you smoke,
and what your weight has been doing. None of it is idle. Each answer changes what is safe, what is
sensible, and sometimes what is possible.

## Then an examination

With a chaperone present if you would like one, and you are welcome to bring somebody with you to
the whole appointment. The examination is specific to what you have come about. For breast or body
surgery it involves measurements, because a plan built on measurements is a plan that can be
explained to you rather than asserted.

Clinical photographs are usually taken at this point. They form part of your record and they are
how a result gets assessed afterwards. Using them for anything beyond your own record, including
this website, is a separate conversation and a separate written consent.

## Then the honest part

Mr Shiatis will tell you what surgery can do about what you have described, and what it cannot.
Those are two different lists and you will hear both. If the honest answer is that an operation
will not give you the result you are after, you will be told that, and it is not a soft no.

If surgery is a reasonable option, you will go through the technique, the scars and where they
fall, the recovery week by week, the risks that are common and the risks that are serious, and the
cost. Written information follows by email so you are not relying on what you remember.

## Nothing is booked on the day

You will be offered a second consultation before any decision, and nothing is reserved or paid for
at the first. This is not a formality. The gap exists so you can read the written information, talk
it over with whoever you talk things over with, think of the questions that only occur to you
afterwards, and come back with them.

A fair number of people use that gap to decide against surgery. That is a good outcome for the
consultation, not a failed one.

## What to bring

- The three things you would most like to change, in your own words
- Any previous surgery, scans or letters relating to what you are asking about
- A list of medicines, supplements and allergies
- Your questions, written down, however small they seem

The last one matters more than people expect. An hour goes quickly, and the question you meant to
ask is always the one you remember in the car.
