Knowing the shape of the day takes a surprising amount of the fear out of it. This is roughly how
it goes, and your own written plan will say where any of it differs for you.

## The morning

You arrive at the time you are given, having not eaten since the hour you were told. Fasting
instructions are not a formality: an operation gets cancelled over a biscuit, and the anaesthetist
makes that call, not the surgeon.

You will be shown to your own room. A nurse goes through your details, your allergies and your
consent form again. Repetition here is deliberate: every check is somebody confirming that the
right operation is about to happen to the right person.

## Marking up

Mr Shiatis will see you before you go anywhere near theatre, standing up and in daylight, and mark
the skin with a surgical pen. These marks are how a plan made at a consultation becomes a plan on
your own anatomy, and they are drawn with you standing because that is how you will be seen
afterwards.

This is also the last easy moment to ask a question. Ask it.

## The anaesthetic

You will meet your anaesthetist beforehand, and they will talk you through what they are giving you
and how you will feel waking up. Some operations here are done under local anaesthetic with
sedation rather than a general, which shortens the day considerably; your plan will say which
applies.

## Afterwards

You wake in recovery, then go back to your room. There will be dressings, possibly a garment, and
somebody checking on you regularly. Most people go home the same evening with a responsible adult
to collect them, which is a requirement rather than a preference.

If an overnight stay would suit you better, or if it turns out to be the sensible thing on the day,
the facility can provide it. Pack a small bag either way: a loose front-fastening top, flat shoes,
your medicines, a phone charger and something to read.

## Before you leave

You go home with written instructions, the medicines you need, a follow-up appointment and a number
to ring. Keep the number somewhere you will find it at three in the morning, which is when people
most often wish they had.
