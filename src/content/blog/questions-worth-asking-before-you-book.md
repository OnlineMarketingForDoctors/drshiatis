Cosmetic surgery in the United Kingdom is less tightly regulated than most people assume. The title
"surgeon" is not protected. A doctor with no plastic surgery training at all can legally advertise
and perform cosmetic operations, and some do.

That puts the burden on you to ask. These are the questions worth asking, and you should ask them
of every surgeon you see, including this one. A good answer is specific and comes without
hesitation.

## 1. Are you on the GMC Specialist Register for Plastic Surgery?

This is the single most useful question. The General Medical Council keeps a public register, and
the specialist list is the one that matters: it means completed, assessed training in the
speciality. You can check any doctor yourself at gmc-uk.org in about a minute, and you should.

## 2. What are your qualifications, and what do the letters mean?

FRCS (Plast) is the UK specialist fellowship examination in plastic surgery, taken at the end of
higher surgical training. It is not the same as FRCS alone, and neither is the same as a diploma in
aesthetic medicine bought over a weekend. Ask what the letters stand for. The answer should be
immediate.

## 3. How often do you do this particular operation?

Not surgery in general. This operation. A surgeon who does your procedure weekly has seen the
variations and the complications; one who does it twice a year has not. You are allowed to ask for
a number.

## 4. Will you be the one operating?

It sounds absurd to have to ask. Ask anyway, and ask who else will be in theatre. Then ask whether
the person you are talking to today will be the person you see at every follow-up.

## 5. Where will the operation happen, and what happens if something goes wrong overnight?

A registered facility with resident medical cover and a route into a hospital bed is not the same
as a treatment room above a shop. Ask what the arrangement is if you need to stay the night, and
ask who you call at two in the morning in the first week.

## 6. What does the quoted price include?

Specifically: the implants or materials, the anaesthetist, the facility fee, the dressings and
garments, every follow-up appointment, and what happens about the cost if a revision turns out to
be needed. A quote that covers only the surgeon's time is not a quote.

## 7. May I see results from your own patients?

Not stock photographs, not results from a group's marketing library. The surgeon's own, including
cases that did not go perfectly. Ask what the complication rate is for this operation in their
hands and how many of their patients have needed a revision.

## 8. What happens if I change my mind?

There should be a cooling-off period between deciding and operating, you should not be asked to pay
a deposit to hold a date at the first appointment, and nobody should be offering you a discount
that expires. Time pressure in cosmetic surgery is a warning sign, without exception.

## And one for yourself

Why now? There is no wrong answer, but there are answers worth examining. Surgery changes how a
body looks. It does not resolve a relationship, a job, or a difficult year, and a surgeon worth
seeing will raise this with you rather than leave it to you to raise.
